## Source boundary and visual reading

The local C34 research record supplies a tall/slight woman, luminous exposed skin, pale face, heavy brows, black eyes and dark maroon hair. E26 supplies a huge leafless glowing tree, red lantern-like fruit and dim grass; it does not establish a hostile botanical queen. B05 and the entire symmetrical garden are invented false-world attachment. Novel chapter URLs are recorded in the local research, not newly browsed or quoted in this review. G03's robe, forehead inset, hairstyle details and palette are costume/design interpretations, not new organs or abilities.

G03 has a recognisable maroon-haired, pale-faced, charcoal-clothed likeness and a daylight/night value pair. G08 provides generous dark mineral ground, pale flowers, repeated shrubs/arches and an intact/stripped pair with the same ground. G10 explicitly places a still non-damageable likeness at the boundary, independent raised/sweeping roots, one exposed low knot and nearby spare ground; its panel7 is more useful for gameplay composition than its large frontal tableaux. G14 panels5/6 contribute quiet luminosity and unsettling symmetry. G17 panel8 labels its garden tether a game addition: grounded bark/root construction, not a sacred source object.

Three adaptations are necessary before production: replace G08's decorative complete floor loops with quiet filled irregular patches so they cannot resemble attack/contact cues; keep raised roots out of the Hero/source/landing projection instead of copying the large foreground arches; use geometric plant stems rather than the board's repeated human-shaped shrubs, preserving exactly one named human likeness. G14's large reaching figure and circular halo are mood studies; the corrected G10 stillness and written non-damageable boundary role take precedence.
