# Act 2 — The Invasion

**Design proposal, researched 8 October 2026.** Five main levels, **45 minutes** of intended first-clear play for someone who completed Act 1, plus three optional levels of **3–5 minutes** each. These are pacing estimates, not timers. None of this campaign content is implemented or playtested.

The reference is H. G. Wells's *The War of the Worlds* (1898). This is a compressed action-game journey through its places and pressures, rather than a literal simulation of its narrator's movements or chronology. The novel's anonymous narrator is a reference character; the game's existing playable character remains the player. The research database distinguishes named people, unnamed roles, machines, places, moods, and proposed gameplay adaptations. [Novel edition and contents](https://etc.usf.edu/lit2go/135/the-war-of-the-worlds/)

## What carries forward

Use Act 1's **five-level scale, short first level, roughly ten-minute ceiling, separately entered optional levels, frequent checkpoints, and interrupted-session resume**. Act 1 supplies no art-direction template for this act. The game's already confirmed portrait presentation, pixelated 2.5D floor movement, camera, controls, one carried weapon, and fixed baseline damage remain campaign-wide constraints.

Act 1 plus this act would target about **88 minutes** before Act 3 and optional content. Playing every proposed Act 2 side level adds about 9–15 minutes, making this act approximately **54–60 minutes**. Time spent reading the optional database, replaying or retrying is outside these estimates. [Act 1 baseline](GAME_CONCEPT.md)

## The experienced player's decision

The repeated decision is: **Which place should I make the machine commit to, and which landing leaves me a useful attack afterwards?**

The playable cycle is **see a machine prepare → swipe to choose a ground landing → the threat locks and commits → dash clear or change sides → tap to strike a reachable recovery point → choose whether the second-tap blast is worth the commitment → read the next warning**. Close-range attacks still matter inside a world dominated by very large machines.

Do not repeat Act 1's direction target, swipe practice or second-tap explanation. The first 30 seconds let the player move and act immediately. New threat rules receive one conspicuous, low-pressure demonstration, then an application, then a combination. An expert's faster recognition shortens those exchanges naturally. It does not unlock a harder gesture mapping.

Progress remains separated:

- **Player mastery:** reading a ray's commitment, keeping an escape landing, managing the screen-space aim anchor, spending recovery on the right enemy, and recognising when a scene has become safe.
- **Attempt state:** current health, weapon, temporary pickups and completed encounters in the current section. A checkpoint retry restores that section's intended starting supplies and machine states; it does not farm rewards.
- **Persistent state:** main/optional level access, saved encounter or boss-phase progress and cosmetic/challenge completion. Optional rewards never make ordinary attacks stronger.

## Small regular enemy roster

Start with **three regular roles**, then two bosses. These regular units are deliberately fictional game adaptations. They are not three species or three named machines authenticated in Wells. Their small scale creates readable close combat; full-size fighting-machines retain their scale in set pieces and the final boss.

| Proposed enemy | First appearance | Windup, commitment and recovery | Decision tested |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Ray Scout** — compact fighting-machine adaptation | Level 1 | A mirror turns toward a short floor lane. The lane stops tracking before the brief ray activates. The mirror then folds back, exposing the near housing at slash range. | Bait a lane, land beside it and punish recovery instead of chasing the aim. |
| **Canister Tender** — invented smoke-logistics unit | Level 3 | Raises a recognisable canister tube, outlines one impact patch, locks it, then fires. A bounded smoke bank expands only to its previewed footprint. The low tube mount remains vulnerable while it reloads. | Remove the source early or accept a temporary loss of floor while attacking another target. |
| **Salvage Handler** — compact handling-machine adaptation | Level 2 | Low five-legged body and articulated tool arms. One arm reaches back, previews a short grab or shove lane, commits, then rests on the floor briefly. | Dash around the committed arm to the exposed tool mount; preserve an exit around the body. |

All three use ordinary slash-range targets. Health, recovery and enemy concurrency are tuning proposals; increasing health is not the main route to greater difficulty. In an initial mixed encounter, allow at most **two actively preparing attacks**, with staggered commitments, and no unavoidable body cage. Start from one or two enemies at a time and widen combinations only when the portrait view supports them. Enemy touching does not apply repeated contact damage. A ray is a brief readable attack, not an offscreen instantaneous kill.

Smoke damage, if retained, should apply after a short visible contact grace and then in bounded ticks rather than one-shotting an accidental edge touch. Do not require standing inside smoke to hit its source. Projectiles do not create random additional clouds; the previewed footprint is the collision footprint. A tender's death does not immediately make existing smoke disappear: its visible dissipation continues, leaving the player to read the floor.

### A2-L1: Horsell Common — Curiosity Burns

**Novel anchor:** the common, pit, cylinder and deputation's destruction produce a sharp change from public curiosity to private panic. [Book I.5](https://etc.usf.edu/lit2go/135/the-war-of-the-worlds/2462/book-onethe-coming-of-the-martians-chapter-5-the-heat-ray/)

**Layout:** three broad heath clearings joined by short road sections around the cylinder pit. Pine trunks and sand heaps frame generous ground pockets. The pit and distant spectators sit beyond the combat boundary; no civilian crowd blocks required dashes. The player skirts the pit rather than descending into it.

| Beat | Budget | Encounter and purpose | Save point |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Arrival and first ray | 2:00 | At most 0:30 of arrival tableau, then movement starts immediately. One Ray Scout aims in an open clearing with two visible alternatives. Let its lane commit, dash beside it and slash the folded housing. A failed first exchange restarts here. | After the scout; about 2:00 |
| Scorched road | 2:00 | Two short encounters, one scout at a time, with the target on a different side. Burned furze marks scenery; only animated lane outlines are hazards. Test a useful attack landing, not just successful evasion. | After the road; about 4:00 |
| Common loop | 2:00 | Choose between two short approaches that rejoin. One has a trunk blocking a direct line; the other has more open floor. Neither requires cover exploitation, a special pickup or a hidden shortcut. A scout in each selected encounter commits once before pressure rises. | At the reunion; about 6:00 |
| Departure clearing | 2:00 | Two scouts take staggered turns from different sides. Clearing them opens the Woking approach. Allow roughly 0:30 within this budget for the cylinder's distant eruption and departure tableau. | Before the finale, then level clear |

**Mastery gain:** the player should recognise that successful evasion can also place them in attack range. No new input appears. Low-pressure demonstrations last one exchange; do not hold experts in a lecture scene or pad the opening with extra waves.

**Production kit:** heath floor, pine trunks, scorched hedges, low sand heaps, cylinder/pit landmarks, road edging, Ray Scout and ray states. The same small ray apparatus supplies the threat grammar later used by the full fighting-machine.

### A2-L2: Weybridge and Shepperton — The Broken Crossing

**Novel anchor:** the riverside exodus, artillery positions and violent fighting-machine advance turn the crossing into a disaster. The narrator's immersion is not adopted as a swimming system. [Book I.12](https://etc.usf.edu/lit2go/135/the-war-of-the-worlds/2469/book-onethe-coming-of-the-martians-chapter-12-what-i-saw-of-the-destruction-of-weybridge-and-shepperton/)

**Layout:** abandoned village street → wide lockside apron → a grounded bridge approach → a sheltered courtyard. Water borders the continuous navigable floor. No tight plank crossing, falling death, boat steering or underwater escape. Civilian movement appears in scenic zones and clears before combat; no escort health bar.

| Beat | Budget | Encounter and purpose | Save point |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Village retreat | 2:00 | One familiar scout between cart-sized obstacles, then a Salvage Handler alone in an open yard. Show the reaching arm's lock and recovery clearly. One slash can exploit each opening. | At the yard exit; about 2:00 |
| Lock apron | 2:30 | First an isolated ground footfall: a nearby visible foot lifts, an outlined patch locks, then the foot lands and withdraws. No player attack is required. Then place one handler beside the same footfall grammar, staggering commitments. | Before the crossing; about 4:30 |
| Broken crossing | 2:30 | Two wide landing pockets connect across continuous ground. Alternate the visible foot's prepared landing between them while a scout uses one short lane. The player selects which threat to bait before crossing to its recovery. The large machine does not chase the camera or add unseen feet. | On the far apron; about 7:00 |
| Shelter approach | 2:00 | A short scout/handler mix, then distant artillery brings down a fighting-machine beyond the boundary. Falling scenic debris does not randomly hit the player. Contact with the shelter entrance ends the level. | Before the final mix, then level clear |

**Mastery gain:** handle two different commitments in one view while preserving a useful landing. This set piece gives scale without spending a third boss budget. No grind through an entire army, and no endless evacuation queue.

**Production kit:** lockside ground, railings, bridge abutments, village façades, carts, abandoned artillery, water edge, handler, one visible giant foot animation and a distant collapse tableau. The foot warning is reused in the final boss.

### A2-L3: Black Smoke and the Ruined House — Under the Machines

**Novel anchors:** Black Smoke suppresses human opposition; the ruined house exposes a handling-machine working beside a cylinder excavation. [Book I.15](https://etc.usf.edu/lit2go/135/the-war-of-the-worlds/2472/book-onethe-coming-of-the-martians-chapter-15-what-had-happened-in-surrey/), [Book II.2](https://etc.usf.edu/lit2go/135/the-war-of-the-worlds/2476/book-twothe-earth-under-the-martians-chapter-2-what-we-saw-from-the-ruined-house/)

**Layout:** a clear suburban road beside bounded smoke banks → an exposed kitchen/scullery floor → a broad excavated work apron. The house's removed front wall keeps the floor and threats visible. Claustrophobia comes from framing and ruined objects; collision space remains generous. Do not spend nine minutes waiting in a cupboard.

| Beat | Budget | Encounter and purpose | Save point |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Smoke edge | 2:00 | A Canister Tender alone previews one cloud with ample clear ground around it. Show the inactive rim, expanding active region and thinning finish. Move around the bank and hit the exposed tube mount. | On clear ground; about 2:00 |
| Ruined house | 2:00 | Two floor approaches around a single low wall. One handler's grab and one tender's patch ask which source to remove first. The tender cannot close both exits; no new canister launches while all available pockets would be blocked. | At the wall opening; about 4:00 |
| Work apron | 2:00 | Brief quiet view of a machine manipulating rods and plates, then a mixed fight with the already introduced roles. Reaching the opened work gate by contact leads to the boss. | Boss entry; about 6:00 |
| Handling-Machine | 3:00 | A compact two-phase fight with one low tool joint and two already previewed arm actions. Aim for about 1:30 per phase; save between them. | Phase transition; about 7:30; then level clear |

**Boss B02 — Handling-Machine:** retain the five-legged, dexterous industrial identity. Phase 1 alternates **Reach** (one short grab lane) and **Place** (a plate lowered onto a previewed patch). The arm plants on the floor after commitment, exposing a tool joint at ordinary slash range. Phase 2 combines the known reach with one bounded smoke patch from a visibly positioned tender at the side. Defeating that tender removes future cloud pressure for this attempt; it does not respawn. The boss then continues its known arm pattern. No additional surprise attack or infinitely replenished helper wave.

The player chooses between spending an opening attacking the machine or removing the tender. Damaging the tool mount eventually disengages the arm from the ruined-house opening and reveals the exit. Do not imply that the player slaughtered an entirely new Martian species. It is one local machine disabled in a gameplay adaptation.

**Mastery gain:** understand area denial without losing basic floor readability; prioritise a source under controlled simultaneous pressure.

**Production kit:** suburban wall sections, kitchen/scullery remnants, exposed foundation floor, sand piles, cylinder panels, rods/plates, smoke/steam states, canister tube, regular handler and boss handler. The boss uses the handler family's body vocabulary, with a distinct silhouette and arm reach.

### A2-L4: Red Weed and the London Approaches — Another Earth

**Novel anchor:** the weed overwhelms watercourses and familiar villas; bleached patches foreshadow its collapse. [Book II.6](https://etc.usf.edu/lit2go/135/the-war-of-the-worlds/2480/book-twothe-earth-under-the-martians-chapter-6-the-work-of-fifteen-days/)

**Layout:** transformed gardens → a dry raised road beside the flooded margin → villa courtyards → a broad Putney approach. Each room has a stable traversable floor; red fronds frame it and make some fixed obstacles visible. Keep wall bases clear enough to distinguish blocking trunks from overhead decoration. The route never requires swimming, climbing or a longer dash.

| Beat | Budget | Encounter and purpose | Save point |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Changed garden | 2:00 | A short quiet reveal, then one handler between two broad frond banks. The banks alter the route shape but do not grow during the attack. Familiar timing in an unfamiliar place establishes confidence. | At the road; about 2:00 |
| Flood margin | 2:30 | A scout aims across a clear strip while a tender denies one side pocket. Use ordinary dashes between multiple dry pockets. Water and decorative fronds never conceal an active floor boundary. | At the next courtyard; about 4:30 |
| Villa sequence | 2:30 | Two compact courtyards rearrange the same three roles, using two at a time. In one, remove the scout to simplify movement; in the other, removing the tender preserves the most useful exit. Include a brief safe view of bleached weed within the budget. | After the second courtyard; about 7:00 |
| Putney approach | 2:00 | A final mixed encounter around an open bridge abutment. The artilleryman can be a safe departure vignette after combat; no digging objective or compulsory conversational wait. Contact with the cleared road ends the level. | Before the mix, then level clear |

**Mastery gain:** choose the next opening from terrain and enemy priorities, using the full familiar roster without introducing another control or faction. Use wider gaps and pressure timing, not merely more health.

**Production kit:** red weed in sparse/dense/bleached states, dry roads, flood margins, broken villas, walls, lamp posts, Putney bridge framing and the existing enemy/effect families. Whitening remains visual foreshadowing, not a collectible upgrade or unexplained plant hazard.

### A2-L5: Dead London and Regent's Park — The Cry Stops

**Novel anchor:** the empty city, stationary howling machines, Regent's Park and Primrose Hill lead to the discovery of Martian death through microbes. [Book II.8](https://etc.usf.edu/lit2go/135/the-war-of-the-worlds/2482/book-twothe-earth-under-the-martians-chapter-8-dead-london/)

**Layout:** two compressed London squares → a park approach → one local combat apron beside the sentry → the redoubt discovery. This is a journey between selected landmarks, not a real-distance map of London. Strong quiet sections make this level feel less crowded than level 4, even though its finale asks for precise mastery.

| Beat | Budget | Encounter and purpose | Save point |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Empty squares | 2:00 | Mostly quiet traversal with black dust, deserted shops and one short familiar encounter. Let the player anticipate danger rather than fill every street with enemies. Keep any combat brief and locally visible. | At the park approach; about 2:00 |
| Regent's approach | 2:00 | One rehearsed footfall, followed by one scout and a reachable clear landing. A wrecked handler beside the road signals occupation failing. The distant cry guides the scene; mechanical warning shapes remain sufficient with sound muted. | Boss entry; about 4:00 |
| Sentry, phase 1 | 2:00 | A ray aimed at the last completed world dash landing; then a committed near-foot placement. Introduce them separately during the first exchanges. The leg joint becomes reachable after either recovery. | Phase transition; about 6:00 |
| Sentry, phase 2 | 2:00 | Combine the familiar ray lane and foot patch with staggered locks. Preserve at least one reachable clear pocket and a useful joint approach. No new attack. Break the local joint brace to open the passage. | Escape route opened; about 8:00 |
| The cry stops | 2:00 | Cross the quiet redoubt by contact thresholds and discover dead Martians and inert machines. Their collapse extends beyond the defeated local sentry. Brief dawn tableau and survivor signal close the act. | Act clear; about 10:00 |

**Boss B05 — Dying Fighting-Machine Sentry:** a full fighting-tripod, already faltering as the invasion's unseen biological failure advances. Its local brace is damaged by the player; its pilot's death and the wider Martian collapse belong to the microbial ending. Avoid a victory line claiming that one sword or blast saved Earth. The three-minute-to-four-minute combat allotment is controlled by meaningful recovery exchanges, not a swollen health bar.

**Exact bait rule:** keep two coordinates separate.

1. **Player attack aim anchor:** the screen-space final finger-release endpoint of the latest swipe, exactly as currently confirmed. Tap direction is computed from that anchor to the tap; the player sprite's world position does not replace it.
2. **Boss bait point:** the player's actual **world-space X/Z landing after the most recent completed dash**, including any collision-shortened landing. A buffered, rejected or not-yet-completed movement does not create a new completed landing for this purpose. Before the first dash after boss entry/checkpoint, initialise the bait point at the player's actual starting floor position.
3. At the start of the ray windup the boss samples that world point, stamps a visible marker there and locks a short ray lane through it. That attack does not retarget when the player next moves. The lane's full active portion, not only the point, is previewed.
4. The player can deliberately dash to a useful bait pocket, see the lock, then dash to a different safe pocket. The dash cooldown and reaction allowance must fit before activation. A slash-range leg joint lowers beside the landing during recovery; choose a tap using the existing screen anchor and optionally add the second-tap blast.
5. Camera movement never converts finger coordinates into this bait point. A screen-edge release may constrain the next tap's aim; leave enough safe floor and recovery time for an ordinary repositioning dash to establish a usable screen anchor.

The boss remains large without making its useful interaction enormous. Lean its hood and mirror apparatus into the **local** view for the ray tell; one attacking foot and the reachable joint occupy the same compact apron. Other supports are scenery during that exchange. If source, warning and safe landing cannot all fit the established camera, recompose the pose and arena. Do not require zooming out, seeing the full silhouette, striking a remote head, circling three distant legs, relying on dash invulnerability or memorising attacks from offscreen.

**Mastery gain:** deliberately control an attack's destination while preserving both escape and close-range retaliation. The quiet coda then resolves the novel's mood rather than immediately spawning another arena.

**Production kit:** London façades/squares, overturned omnibus, park gates and paths, bleached weed, black dust, wrecked handling-machine, full fighting-machine hood/mirror/near-leg states, redoubt piles, inert machinery and dawn. A flying-machine may appear only as a static redoubt discovery; it is not a compulsory additional boss.

## Three optional levels

Each is selected separately after its parent level, with one checkpoint before its final exercise. Finish by contact with an opened exit. Each uses ordinary dashes, baseline slash damage and the current weapon. Rewards are an invasion-themed cosmetic or completion stamp; none is required for Act 3. No new control, environment kit or enemy family is introduced.

| Optional level | Parent / target | Reused kit | Few additions | Different decision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Cylinder Perimeter** | A2-L1 / 3–5 min (start at 3) | Heath clearings, cylinder/pit edge, trunks, Ray Scouts and lane warnings | Three journal fragments collected by contact; one exit marker | Choose the order around a compact loop so the next ray is baited away from a useful return route. No repeated long walk to the same pit. |
| **Clear Air Circuit** | A2-L3 / 3–5 min (start at 4) | Work apron, ruined-house walls, canister tubes, tenders, one handler, smoke states | Two distinctive empty/full supply-crate states; one completed-circuit marker | Clear two tender positions in either order, then defeat the final handler. Spend openings on area-denial sources instead of the largest body. The player does not acquire a gas-mask control or build a smoke-clearing device. |
| **Bleached Canal** | A2-L4 / 3–5 min (start at 5) | Weed/bleached weed, dry road, flooded margins, villa walls, scouts and handlers | Three route signs and one small canal-side scenic prop | Three short arrangements use different broad dry pockets. Read the same tells from a different approach and preserve a return landing. Bleached vegetation is scenery; no surprise collapse or underwater section. |

Place ordinary restorable supplies at the saved restart state. Cosmetic credit is awarded once for completion, not for repeatedly dying after a pickup. If a temporary dash-speed, dash-length or 360-swing experiment is offered, baseline completion must remain practical and its effect ends according to the separately tested campaign rules. Optional levels are not a prerequisite grind.

## Mood through play

The mood arc is a design reading of the novel: **curiosity and rupture → frantic collective retreat → confinement and methodical occupation → human displacement → lonely suspense and uncertain relief**. The research mood catalogue contains source-specific evidence; this proposal translates those moods into encounter density and breathing time.

- Level 1 keeps a brief populated opening and abruptly empty aftermath. The player already knows how to move; the disturbing discovery concerns the world.
- Level 2 has the most apparent public activity and external scale, with short safe pauses between crossings.
- Level 3 narrows the framing and repeats industrial motions while keeping its actual combat floor fair.
- Level 4 uses changed landmarks and quieter links to show that resistance has become survival.
- Level 5 reduces ordinary enemy density, lets absence and the cry carry suspense, then holds two minutes for discovery and relief after the local escape fight.

Quiet beats are contained within each time budget. They offer immediate navigation or a short tableau; they are not compulsory idle timers. Optional journals can carry cast details without blocking the main route. This is not an invitation to replicate Act 1's theatrical palette or scenery.

## Shared fairness and mobile rules

- Let **D** be the normal dash distance; the present prototype uses 2.7 world units. Begin local combat clearings around two dash lengths across, with broad landing pockets and required gaps about one dash length wide. Verify these dimensions in the existing portrait camera rather than turning them into fixed production measurements. [Current baseline](GAME_CONCEPT.md)
- Every damaging exchange must show its warning, local attack source and a reachable safe landing together. The delay covers recognition, any remaining dash cooldown and movement. Warnings, active damage and recovery differ by outline, motion and fill as well as brightness.
- Floor warnings stay on top of decorative smoke/fronds/dust. Smoke cannot hide the next tell or every escape pocket. Check the union of active footprints before scheduling another attack; defer it if it would seal the arena. Required paths never cross an already active damaging region.
- No ordinary body cage, no damage for brushing a civilian, no offscreen shot, no unmarked falling debris, and no lethal scenic water or pit-edge knockback. Continuous floor and tolerant thresholds support interrupted phone play.
- Camera framing and enemy pose solve scale. No boss requires a changed camera, a ranged-only weak point or an impossible tap beyond the display. All bosses must be viable with slashes only, fixed damage, ordinary dash length and no powerup.
- Save after meaningful completed encounters, before finales and after each boss phase. Target 1–3 minutes of replay exposure. A checkpoint restores coherent floor hazards and boss state; it never reloads directly into an active ray.
- Backgrounding freezes the entire simulation. On return, show the frozen situation with a resume overlay; the tap resumes play and is consumed by the overlay rather than becoming a slash. Warnings continue from their saved progress after resume, without an invisible jump in time.
- First-clear time includes short transitions and required scenes. If experts finish a level faster, accept the improvement; add challenge through new arrangements only when it creates a useful decision. Do not pad a target time with repeated waves, doors that demand waiting or mandatory scavenger chores.

## Small tests before level production

Test four short greyboxes with people who can already complete Act 1. Hold player stats, weapon and normal dash distance constant. Record aiming mistakes separately from positioning, warning recognition and mistimed dashes.

| Test | Hypothesis | Observe / revise when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| One scout and two landing pockets | An experienced player understands the ray commitment after one visible exchange | They evade by luck but cannot predict the locked lane; enlarge or simplify the warning rather than add a tutorial lecture |
| Tender + handler on a work apron | Smoke creates target-priority decisions while leaving a viable baseline route | Players feel forced into gas, cannot see a tell, or always remove the same source without evaluating the floor; revise footprint, concurrency or layout |
| Final sentry bait and joint opening | Players can explain and deliberately use the last completed **world** dash landing | Camera movement or finger-release position appears to move the marker; fix coordinate handling. Test collision-shortened landings, buffered dashes and checkpoint initialisation |
| Local boss + mobile interruption | The source, warning, safe landing and weak point stay readable at current zoom | Players need the whole tripod onscreen, an offscreen tap, or invulnerability. Recompose the local pose; test muted audio, edge swipes and backgrounding during a warning |

For every boss, attempt slash-only completion, then ordinary slash-plus-blast completion without pickups. Try all four display edges as swipe-release anchors. Resume during windup, active smoke and a phase transition; verify that progress, cooldown and hazards remain coherent. Initial success should come from better decisions with identical stats. Once these tests support the loop, time a complete level with transitions and revise the 45-minute estimate from observed play; the estimate is not evidence of finished balance.
