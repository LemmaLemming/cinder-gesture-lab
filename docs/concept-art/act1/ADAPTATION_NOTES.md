# Source and game boundaries

The new collection develops the existing researched Act 1 direction. Read the [film-linked style guide](../../reference-library/act1/STYLE_GUIDE.md) for observed shapes and the [level proposal](../../ACT1_CONCEPT.md) for combat rules.

Film evidence supports period astronomy costumes, workers and launch crew; a squat finless shell; jagged painted lunar scenery; theatrical celestial people; oversized umbrella-cap fungi; masked upright Selenites; a royal court of crescents, radial roundels, curling panels and drapery; escape, harbour rescue and celebration tableaux. The monochrome game palette is our choice: surviving handcoloured film material is documented separately. Later drawings and the 1960 Selenite reconstruction remain dated as later work.

Character likenesses, costume variants, exact room layouts and all warning/weak-point diagrams are proposals. The six expedition costume studies do not establish that each named professor has an identifiable screen costume. The Rush, Swarm and Spear Guard are three game roles drawn from one film costume family. Moon combat, King's lunge and finite guard-pair pattern are inventions. The traveller's blade and blast translate the gesture system rather than reconstructing film combat.

The player's first act introduces one readable rule at a time. Every required fight and boss opening must work with ordinary dashes and slashes. Friendly humans and celestial dream performers are scenery. The mushroom effect is player-triggered harmless repulsion: living enemies retreat, then regroup when the spores fade. Automatic damaging vents in the earlier G02 environment study are superseded.

Development boards combine full scenes, silhouettes, module studies and schematic diagrams. Decorative captions are generated lettering, never historical quotations. Extra moon-rock stage flats on the Earth board are a stylistic proposal rather than a claim that the film's observatory had lunar scenery. Optional layouts reuse their parent kit. No diagram implies jumping, climbing, mandatory narrow bridges, interaction buttons, damage upgrades or a new enemy faction.

The three game views are concept mockups with no HUD. They do not verify playable geometry, camera occlusion, melee reach, dash cooldown or warning time. Production should first test the five short greyboxes listed in the level proposal. When art and rules diverge, the written design and film-linked database govern the next revision.

The three legacy PNGs at this folder's root remain the superseded exploratory pass. Six researched earlier studies remain in `docs/reference-library/act1/concepts/`. The new manifest assigns G07–G26 to twenty additional final images, keeping G01–G06 and O01–O70 stable.
