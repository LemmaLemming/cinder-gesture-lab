# Act 2 — The Invasion: concept-art collection

Original game concepts inspired by H. G. Wells's *The War of the Worlds* (1898), researched and developed 8 October 2026. These are planning illustrations, not screenshots of the current Godot prototype or finished production assets.

## Three possible game views

1. [Horsell Common](gameplay/01-horsell-common-gameplay.png): familiar gestures under new aiming pressure, with a cylinder and scorched common scenery.
2. [The ruined house](gameplay/02-ruined-house-gameplay.png): a five-legged handling machine commits a sweep; the player attacks its reachable actuator.
3. [Dead London](gameplay/03-dead-london-gameplay.png): a fighting tripod locks onto a previous physical dash landing, exposing a low joint after commitment.

These views retain portrait orientation, a close fixed-angle overhead camera, pixel sprites against blocky 2.5D scenery, dash-only movement, close-range attacks and one carried weapon. The selected rust-red, black and pale-ash treatment develops Act 2's existing palette proposal. Act 1 supplies the five-level timing/checkpoint/optional-level framework; its lunar theatrical art does not supply this act's environment design.

## Source and adaptation boundaries

The source describes round-bodied Martians with sixteen tentacles, flexible jointed fighting tripods, a separate five-legged handling machine, cylinders, an invisible Heat-Ray, Black Smoke canisters, and red weed that later dies of disease. The novel's invasion ends through terrestrial microbes. See the [research sources](../../reference-library/act2/research/SOURCES.md) and each database record's chapter references.

Character faces, exact costume colours, mechanical cutaways, arena layouts, low melee openings and pale attack warnings are visual/game interpretations. The novel's invisible Heat-Ray gains visible floor telegraphs in the game so the player can anticipate danger. Compact regular enemy variants are invented adaptations. Unselected candidate bosses are possibilities, not extra required levels. Human refugees and NPCs are not enemy factions. Art may simplify machine articulation and crowd figures; the database and level document remain the source of the exact design rules.
