# Act I film research: The Moon

Research date: **8 October 2026**. The source is Georges Méliès’s *Le Voyage dans la Lune / A Trip to the Moon* (**1902**). The existing film stills, production photographs and 70 object observations provide the visual foundation. New research adds role, environment, boss, mood and prop-kit indexes without replacing that evidence.

The film is short; its practical cast database should cover identifiable story roles and ensembles without fabricating names for every person in a crowd. There are **89 research/design entities**: **35 character/role records, 17 environments, 5 boss candidates, 12 mood interpretations and 20 prop/effect kits**. The character total includes repeated scene functions of the same Selenite population and three invented regular enemy variants. It is not a claim that the film contains 35 unique named people.

## Story, naming and cast

The expedition follows an academic meeting, handmade industrial preparation, a cannon launch, impossible lunar spectacle, giant fungi, an encounter with the lunar court, escape and public celebration. These episodes support a varied act: amusing ceremony, curiosity, brief discomfort, acrobatic danger, royal farce, urgency and relief. The [official Cannes record](https://www.festival-cannes.com/f/le-voyage-dans-la-lune/) confirms the six-traveller party and return framing; the [restorer’s story guide](https://melies.lobsterfilms.com/contes.php) supplies names and scene-event labels.

The six story-guide expedition names are **Barbenfouillis, Nostradamus, Alcofrisbas, Omega, Micromegas and Parafaragaramus**. The restorer spells the third **Alcofribras**; aliases are retained rather than silently standardized across sources. These names are not evidence that the five companions possess five distinct classes, powers or playable identities. No reliable one-to-one mapping of companion names to the five actor credits is established in this research.

The [Cinémathèque film record](https://www.cinematheque.fr/film/52565.html) supplies **11 named performers and 2 ensembles**. The [cast index](cast-index.json) retains all of them, with published role attributions. Méliès is credited as Barbenfouillis and the Moon; Bernon as Phoebé; Delannoy as the shell pilot; Lallement as the officer; Legris as the parade leader; d’Alcy as secretary, star and shell operator. Five other credits say only astronomer. Actor identity, fictional character name, and visible position within a saved frame remain separate fields. The pilot attribution does not create a seventh expedition scientist.

The celestial figures receive similarly careful labels. The visible crescent woman is called Phoebe in the restorer’s guide and Phoebé in institutional credits. MoMA’s later drawing title says **Phoebus**; that title discrepancy is preserved. The ringed-globe figure is supported as Saturn. The robed figure beneath the Earth globe remains unnamed; assigning Jupiter or Atlas from a silhouette would overstate the evidence. Seven face-bearing stars, the paired star figures and the planetary tableau are not collapsed into a single identical composition.

## What a new player should experience

The [Act I level plan](../../../ACT1_CONCEPT.md) retains the established five main levels and **6, 8, 9, 10, 10 minute** first-clear targets: **43 minutes**. These are design estimates that include normal travel and brief transitions, not playtest measurements or timers. Optional Salvage Circuit, Spore Bloom and Royal Rehearsal use their parent kits and separate 3–5 minute ranges; all-content guidance is **52–58 minutes**, excluding retries and deliberate replays.

L1 is a safe observatory/launch rehearsal. It teaches a useful dash landing, swipe-end-relative attack aim, immediate tap slash, the second-tap blast, and contact objectives. L2 introduces the first live rush commitment. L3 introduces the swarm, hittable spore repulsion and later spear grammar. L4 combines familiar guards and the first major boss. L5 tests learned responses around the Moon-face encounter and escape. This keeps the act’s shorter opening consistent with Act II/III’s encounter budgets without assuming a returning player already knows the gestures.

The adaptation remains portrait pixel 2.5D, with a close fixed-angle player-following camera. Swipes cause ground-plane dashes; there is no walk stick or separate jump. Every required path works with normal dash length. A tap immediately slashes; a quick second tap chains one short-range blast; aim is from the latest swipe’s final release to the tap location. Carry one weapon and keep baseline damage fixed. More ornate costume does not imply permanent stat growth.

The art should make each warning, threat origin, useful landing and reachable recovery target visible together. Short square-particle puffs translate stage magic while preserving the collision floor. A large creature may extend above the view, but the next relevant warning may not depend on unseen animation.

## Visual continuity and mood

Use the [existing style guide](../STYLE_GUIDE.md): painted-looking wings and backdrop layers, jagged rocks with broad white marks, dark negative space, hats and exaggerated beards, the finless squat shell, shallow giant mushroom caps, masked upright rib-banded Selenites, and court crescents, radial discs, curling panels and drapery. The earlier sealed spacesuit, crawling beetle, finned rocket and Gothic insect cathedral images remain preserved exploratory revisions.

Moon-white, dusty silver and black are **project art direction**. Authentic hand-coloured prints are separately tagged; the 2011 colour restoration and its modern score are not evidence for a universal 1902 monochrome palette or an original soundtrack. D01–D12 map a mood arc from academic burlesque through handmade spectacle, anticipation, wonder, dream pageantry, discomfort, fungal discovery, acrobatic peril, royal farce, urgency, relief and civic comedy. Do not make every chamber a dense hostile wave.

The grotto should communicate the approved interaction: **hittable clusters shed falling spores that temporarily repel swarmers**. Glowing pickup mushrooms, square spores, spore inventory, warning glyphs, practice bells, salvage collection and contact markers are game additions. Broad new environment-interactable systems remain deferred until enemy behaviour warrants them.

## Provenance qualifications

- Original film imagery, production photographs, surviving costume, later drawings and later reconstruction retain their different kinds and dates.
- MoMA scene drawings are catalogued c.1930–31. The Cinémathèque Selenite drawing is 1937; Labisse’s reconstruction is 1960. None becomes 1902 preproduction art by being used as a reference.
- The original astronomer robe supports the academic costume; it does not prove that explorers wear it throughout the journey.
- The source retelling’s tableau headings, museum drawing scene numbers and saved-frame IDs are different numbering systems. No exact shot count, frame timestamp, projection speed or original-release runtime is inferred from their agreement.
- Research uses museum/official film records and direct image citations. No additional museum image pixels were copied. The Ciclic PDF’s search-index cast extract remains a qualified supplemental source; the complete Cinémathèque and Cannes HTML records provide the stronger cast basis.
- Generated studies and the three proposed game views are concepts, not production assets or screenshots of an implemented campaign.
