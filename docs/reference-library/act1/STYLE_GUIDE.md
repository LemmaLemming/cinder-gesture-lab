# Act 1 — Méliès lunar theatre

Act 1 takes its visual vocabulary from Georges Méliès' *Le Voyage dans la Lune* (1902): theatrical scenery, astronomical symbols, Victorian expedition clothes, giant fungi, and masked humanoid Selenites. These are planning and art-direction notes. The portrait camera, gesture combat, boss encounters, optional routes, and square particles are game adaptations.

## Eighteen visual rules

1. **Compose scenery in theatrical layers.** Use a readable foreground, side scenery, a middle ground for characters, and a painted-looking backdrop. In the game, sprite layers and shallow scenery can suggest depth around a clear collision floor.
2. **Give lunar rocks irregular, jagged outlines.** The film's scenery is strongly graphic. Translate its broken silhouettes into a small set of reusable rock flats instead of a wall of regular basalt columns.
3. **Paint light and dark into large shapes.** Broad white streaks and dark creases carry the rock form. Keep pixel clusters deliberate; dense uniform texture should not compete with enemies or warnings.
4. **Preserve black space between scenery.** Dark sky and openings separate bright rocks, costumes, and astronomical shapes. Keep busy scenery outside the immediate combat floor. These four rules interpret the visible staging in the [lunar exterior](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:M%C3%A9li%C3%A8s,_viaggio_nella_luna_(1902)_07.jpg) and [celestial dream](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:M%C3%A9li%C3%A8s,_viaggio_nella_luna_(1902)_08.jpg) film frames.
5. **Use large stars, crescents, and discs.** Their strong geometric silhouettes establish the film's lunar fantasy more clearly than generic machinery. They can repeat across scenery, entrances, and court ornament.
6. **Personify the heavens through theatrical human figures.** A woman on a crescent and a figure within Saturn's ringed globe are costume-and-prop images. Retain expressive human poses rather than translating every celestial figure into an abstract monster. The [dream frame](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:M%C3%A9li%C3%A8s,_viaggio_nella_luna_(1902)_08.jpg) and the user's supplied still are the direct references; MoMA's [later dream drawing](https://www.moma.org/collection/works/38324) is a separate composition.
7. **Dress travellers as period astronomers.** Coats, beards, and brimmed hats are central to the expedition silhouette. A sealed modern spacesuit and dark visor would lose this vocabulary. See the [landing frame](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:M%C3%A9li%C3%A8s,_viaggio_nella_luna_(1902)_07.jpg).
8. **Reserve academic robes and pointed hats for the scientific ceremony or an explicit costume adaptation.** The surviving robe has esoteric embroidery; the museum places it in the first two tableaux. Do not assume the travellers wear that same robe throughout the journey. [Cinémathèque original costume record](https://www.cinematheque.fr/objet/392.html).
9. **Keep umbrellas and canes in the expedition's prop vocabulary.** They are recognizable period shapes. A sword, short-range blast, or umbrella-inspired weapon remains a proposed game interpretation. Do not present the current slash-and-blast system as an original film action. See the [grotto frame](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:M%C3%A9li%C3%A8s,_viaggio_nella_luna_(1902)_09.jpg).
10. **Use a squat, finless bullet-shaped capsule.** The film's shell has a rounded nose and cylindrical body. Avoid the tall finned rocket from the earlier concept. Reuse the shell for launch, landing, and escape compositions. [Escape frame](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:M%C3%A9li%C3%A8s,_viaggio_nella_luna_(1902)_12.jpg).
11. **Keep mushrooms simple and oversized.** Shallow umbrella-like caps, long stalks, and varied scale make the grotto legible. Square spore hazards and glowing pickup mushrooms are optional game additions, not authenticated film objects.
12. **Make the grotto an irregular scenic chamber.** Use rock shelving, a trunk-like crossing, and fungi around a broad floor. A built stone-tile dungeon changes its character. Both rules use the [grotto film frame](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:M%C3%A9li%C3%A8s,_viaggio_nella_luna_(1902)_09.jpg); the [c.1930–31 grotto drawing](https://www.moma.org/collection/works/38318) offers later variations rather than an exact original plan.
13. **Make Selenites humanoid stage creatures.** The film court shows biped figures. The Cinémathèque identifies the original performers as acrobats from the Folies Bergère. Acrobatic poses can inform enemy animation, while timing and combat rules remain proposals. [Court frame](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:M%C3%A9li%C3%A8s,_viaggio_nella_luna_(1902)_10.jpg), [museum reconstruction record and performer context](https://www.cinematheque.fr/objet/1540.html).
14. **Use rib bands and projecting headpieces to identify Selenites.** Preserve the pale torso pattern and antenna-like head silhouette at pixel scale. Crawling skull-faced beetles belong to the earlier game interpretation. Do not infer original materials or exact colours from Labisse's later sculpture. [Court frame](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:M%C3%A9li%C3%A8s,_viaggio_nella_luna_(1902)_10.jpg).
15. **Build the court from celestial ornament and drapery.** Its crescents, radiant roundels, curling panels, spearheads, and curtained opening provide a reusable kit. Large Gothic stone arches, heraldic beetle statues, and rows of magical lamps are additions rather than film references.
16. **Stage the royal court broadly across the screen.** The royal area sits to one side of a populated tableau. Adapt this as an open encounter with a compact throne landmark, avoiding an obligatory long cathedral aisle. These court rules come from the [original court frame](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:M%C3%A9li%C3%A8s,_viaggio_nella_luna_(1902)_10.jpg).
17. **Use bright puffs and transformations to convey stage magic.** The visible white cloud in the [escape frame](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:M%C3%A9li%C3%A8s,_viaggio_nella_luna_(1902)_11.jpg) is a useful effect reference. Square smoke and gravity-driven square debris are the game's interpretation. A still does not establish animation duration, trigger, or particle physics.
18. **Translate the tableau into portrait gameplay through framing.** Retain painted side scenery, oversized props, and a readable character silhouette around a broad playable floor. The close following camera is a game requirement; a brief wider reveal may introduce the Moon's face, but combat warnings must remain visible in the following view. This is a proposed adaptation of the film's visual staging, not a claim about its camera system.

## Corrections to the earlier concepts

The three earlier generated images remain exploratory game concepts. They are not historical concept art or film frames.

| Earlier choice | Closer film direction |
| --- | --- |
| Helmeted armoured traveller with a black visor | Bearded period astronomer; coat and hat silhouette |
| Crawling skull-faced insects | Masked biped Selenites with pale rib bands and projecting headpieces |
| Tall finned rocket | Finless bullet-shaped capsule |
| Regular basalt walls and evenly scattered rock texture | Jagged scenery outlines with broad painted light and shadow |
| Tiled fungal dungeon and glowing vents | Irregular grotto, cap-and-stalk mushrooms, rock shelves, trunk crossing |
| Gothic insect cathedral, beetle statues, magical lamps | Curling court panels, radiant roundel, crescents, spearheads, drapery |

The earlier images already explore portrait composition and an open combat floor. Preserve those useful game-layout ideas while replacing the scenery and costume vocabulary.

## Palette and print versions

Moon-white, dusty silver, and black remain the chosen Act 1 game palette. It is an interpretation of black-and-white film imagery, not the only historically authentic colour scheme. A surviving hand-coloured print and its modern restoration also provide authentic reference material. MoMA's [2011 programme announcement](https://press.moma.org/wp-content/files_mf/tsap_pressrelease_final.pdf) describes the colour restoration by Lobster Films, the Groupama Gan Foundation, and the Technicolor Foundation. The library's [coloured monument frame](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Trip_to_the_Moon_Statue_Color.jpg) is identified as a frame from that hand-coloured print.

Tag the source version on each reference. Do not silently mix a modern invented palette, a black-and-white still, and a restored hand-coloured frame into a single asserted original palette. Any coloured accents added to the monochrome game should be documented as game art direction.

## Portrait and gesture constraints

- Design for a vertical phone display and a close, fixed-angle camera following the player.
- Swiping dashes in any direction and is the only voluntary movement. Every required passage and landing area must work with the normal dash; a dash-length pickup must not be necessary to finish a required route.
- A tap slashes immediately. A quick second tap adds one short-range blast. Art and animation should distinguish those actions without adding another slash or a weapon-switching control.
- Aim is the direction from the final endpoint of the latest swipe to the tap location. Preserve that screen-relative rule; scenery and effects must not obscure the gesture landing or attack feedback.
- Carry one weapon. A pickup replaces the previous weapon; there is no weapon-switching hotbar. Distinct weapons need readable silhouettes within the same short-range combat scale.
- Keep baseline damage fixed. Visual progression comes through new places, enemies, boss behaviour, and situational powerups rather than increasingly ornate armour implying permanent stat growth.
- Place hazard warnings and nearby threats within the portrait camera. Use clear silhouette and value separation rather than colour alone. Keep square particles short-lived and sparse enough to preserve attack readability; those details need gameplay tuning.

## Optional levels share their parent kit

Optional routes and rooms use the regular level's floor treatment, rock flats, mushrooms, court panels, props, enemy sprites, and palette. Their distinctiveness comes from rearrangement and a small number of additions: a unique landmark, a local hazard, a replacement weapon, a temporary powerup, or a different encounter pattern. These functions are proposals and must be labelled separately from observed film objects.

Examples: an optional grotto rearranges the same cap-and-stalk mushrooms and shelves, then adds one clearly marked spore mechanism; an optional court reuses the same curtain arch, crescent columns, and guards, then adds a ceremonial challenge prop. Avoid making a separate tileset, new faction, or new visual era for each optional room. The escape and celebration scenes can also reuse the existing capsule, Selenite, astronomer, and sailor-attendant assets.

## Historical provenance

Keep the following kinds separate in the database and gallery:

| Reference kind | What can be established | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Original film frame | What is visible in that source print and captured moment | F01–F13 and the coloured celebration still |
| Surviving original object | The specific object's museum identity and documented relation to the film | [Astronomer robe, 1902](https://www.cinematheque.fr/objet/392.html) |
| Later drawing by Méliès | His later reinterpretation of a scene; not automatically a preproduction design | MoMA [grotto](https://www.moma.org/collection/works/38318), [dream](https://www.moma.org/collection/works/38324), [Moon-eye](https://www.moma.org/collection/works/38327), and [Earthrise](https://www.moma.org/collection/works/36891) drawings, catalogued c.1930–31; Cinémathèque [Selenites drawing, 1937](https://www.cinematheque.fr/objet/400.html) |
| Later museum reconstruction | A later artist's interpretation, with its own date and materials | [Félix Labisse's red painted papier-mâché Selenite, 1960](https://www.cinematheque.fr/objet/1540.html), based on the 1937 drawing |
| New game concept or object proposal | A contemporary adaptation for this project | Generated level art, pixel redesigns, boss attacks, hazards, pickups, and optional-room additions |

Do not relabel a retrospective drawing as a 1902 production sketch or assume the reconstruction's material and colour describe the original costume. The artist's later drawings can also rearrange a composition: the MoMA dream drawing and the original dream still are visibly different. Preserve source titles, catalogue dates, source links, and uncertainty in individual records. Scene labels are sufficient here; no unverified timestamps are assigned.
