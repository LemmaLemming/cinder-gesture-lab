## Two primary-source examples

### 1. A Short Hike: readable regions and correcting reverse exploration

[Adam Robinson-Yu, developer account on PlayStation Blog, 13 November 2021](https://blog.playstation.com/2021/11/13/the-award-winning-a-short-hike-hits-ps4-november-16/). Inspected the article body, specifically paragraphs about terrain/landmark formation, freedom of exploration, the region map and players exploring the back of the mountain first (browser text lines 60–71).

Observation: the creator differentiates areas through terrain shapes and recognisable landmarks. Testing exposed players approaching from the back; a cave helped reconnect that approach with important content. This is evidence for checking alternate approaches, not proof that a single prescribed route is sufficient.

Proposed Cinder application: keep the existing capsule/hatch recognisable from the three part pockets and return approaches, using parent flats to distinguish pockets without making one collection order mandatory. Proposed local check: brief real portrait views from each pocket and both return directions; verify a visible local way back and that scenic silhouettes do not mask contact parts, Hero feet, source, warning footprint or useful landing. No stamina progression, cave, climbing or new control is proposed.

Access: actual HTML article text opened and read. Embedded footage/GIFs were not watched; no footage claim.

### 2. Super Mario 3D Land: collection tempo and honest threat placement

[Nintendo, Iwata Asks, Volume 1 section 6, “It's Fun, So It's Okay!”](https://www.nintendo.com/en-gb/Iwata-Asks/Iwata-Asks-SUPER-MARIO-3D-LAND/Vol-1-SUPER-MARIO-3D-LAND/6-It-s-Fun-So-It-s-Okay-/6-It-s-Fun-So-It-s-Okay--217087.html). Inspected the automatic-coin/backtracking discussion (browser text lines 28–57) and the enemy-after-coin placement discussion (lines 107–120).

Observation: Hayashida and Iwata discuss unnecessary backtracking for dropped coins disrupting movement tempo. Hayashida also describes correcting hostile placement immediately after a collectible. Collection should not conceal a surprise threat.

Proposed Cinder application: retain the already-required contact pickup, with collected feedback during normal movement and familiar visible rusher/impact warnings before danger. Proposed local check: contact one part with an ordinary swipe, confirm its collected state once, continue without an extra button or mandatory dwell, and inspect a nearby warning/lock plus reachable onward or return landing. Repeat contact must not recollect or multiply reward. This does not propose automatic remote collection, enemy-drop coins, checkpoint growth or changed attacks.

Access: actual official UK HTML interview text opened and read. The US archive returned 502 on direct open; the UK page supplied the inspected text. Embedded videos were not watched.
