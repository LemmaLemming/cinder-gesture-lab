## 1. Readable sequence, useful recovery: Furi

**Primary source:** [Emeric Thoa, creative director at The Game Bakers — “Furi on PS4: A Gauntlet of Brutal Boss Battles”](https://blog.playstation.com/2016/03/09/furi-on-ps4-a-gauntlet-of-brutal-boss-battles/), 9 March 2016.

**Inspected section:** article body, paragraphs beginning “Furi is heavily inspired,” “The gameplay in Furi,” and “The challenges are mostly based” (retrieved lines 60–66).

**Observation:** Thoa links immediate input response with fairness, and describes depth through responding to different opponents with a small existing move set. His attack loop explicitly connects advance warning, reaction and the opportunity to punish the opponent’s weakness.

**Tentative application:** keep the guards’ existing warning → lock → active → recovery grammar. Change which gap and flank are useful across the three formations; the payoff for a good dash should be a reachable ordinary-primary opening. This supports spatial variation, not new controls or shorter tells.

**Narrow local test:** in one parallel-pair and one inward-pair attack cycle, record the actual warning sources, locked footprints, reachable ordinary-dash landing and subsequent primary opening. Check source, footprint and landing together in the portrait view. Distinguish simulated/positioned fixture evidence from actual gesture execution. This is a focused cycle check, not a full-route gate.

## 2. Combinations can challenge without hidden information: Into the Breach

**Primary interview:** [Justin Ma of Subset Games — “Road to the IGF: Subset Games’ Into the Breach”](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/road-to-the-igf-subset-games-i-into-the-breach-i-), interviewed by Joel Couture, 23 February 2018.

**Inspected sections:** the answer about lessons from FTL; “The enemies telegraph their movements…”; and the final paragraph of “What was key…” (retrieved lines 65, 69–71 and 78). These are Ma’s answers, not an external gameplay analysis.

**Observation:** Ma treats visible intentions as the basis for fresh tactical problems. He also warns that adding a single enemy can make a manageable battle impossible, and values a failure whose cause the player can understand.

**Tentative application:** first inspect overlapping spear lanes and the resulting useful gap. Make the inward pair require a different approach using the same guard cast and timings. Transfer only the readable spatial-choice principle from this turn-based game; it supplies no evidence for Cinder’s real-time reaction budget.

**Narrow local test:** sample each existing formation’s actual committed lane combination and verify at least one reachable safe landing with an ordinary close-range follow-up. Flag a gap that only looks clear but is covered by the other guard’s active footprint, or that leaves no attack opportunity. Do not solve such a failure by adding enemies, ammo requirements or dash invulnerability dependence.

## 3. Preserve progress while retaining the exercise: Save Game Systems

**Primary designer breakdown:** [David Sirlin — “Save Game Systems”](https://www.sirlin.net/articles/save-game-systems), dated 9 August 2014 on the author’s site. Sirlin analyzes other games here; this is not a production account from the makers of those games.

**Inspected sections:** opening argument about meaningful progress and the complete “Save Point vs. Checkpoint” section (retrieved lines 27–42).

**Observation:** Sirlin separates the convenience of resuming after an interruption from the challenge within an encounter. He argues that preserving an achieved checkpoint can coexist with demanding play.

**Tentative application:** preserve O3’s authored checkpoint before the final arrangement, so practice of that arrangement does not require repeating the two completed exercises. Keep a clear quiet state in which the contact bell starts the next exercise deliberately. This proposes no change to the shared save system.

**Narrow local test:** capture the genuine pre-final checkpoint, enter the final exercise, and use the production Retry control. Verify restoration to that checkpoint, the first two formations still cleared, the final formation unstarted and the bell available. Separately verify a public Save/fresh Continue preserves the actual saved exercise state without silently starting an attack. Report real restoration cost; this article establishes no acceptable latency number.
